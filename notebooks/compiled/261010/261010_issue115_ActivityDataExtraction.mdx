## Introduction

Accurate and scalable aggregation of plastic-degrading enzyme activity data from academic literature is limited by the bottleneck of manual human curation found in resources like PAZY[cite: 1]. To overcome this constraint, I developed an automated, high-precision extraction pipeline powered by large language models to parse thousands of academic PDFs[cite: 1]. The system extracts activity measurements specifically for experimentally validated plastic-degrading enzymes while filtering out unverified targets[cite: 1].

The pipeline utilizes an iterative multi-pass extraction architecture and targeted LLM verification passes to eliminate hallucinations, enforce entity grounding and recover missing values[cite: 1]. Synthetic controls and cross-model benchmarks were developed to quantify extraction precision, recall and relational integrity prior to full-scale deployment across literature corpora from the previous phase scraping[cite: 1].

## Objectives

1. Engineer a multi-stage LLM extraction and verification pipeline using Claude Sonnet on AWS Bedrock to extract enzyme and experimental assay parameters with verbatim evidence grounding[cite: 2].
2. Generate spiked synthetic controls within real paper to assess true positive capture rates, decoy rejection rates and relational pool linkage[cite: 2].
3. Benchmark LLM models that I have access to (Sonnet, Haiku, Opus and hybrid configurations of the three) against a cross-model consensus truth pool to determine optimal accuracy and cost[cite: 2].

### Processing Steps

**Phase 1: Multi-Stage Extraction and Verification Pipeline Development**[cite: 2]
- Pass 1: Sonnet 4.6 generates a dossier of all the plastic degrading enzymes in the paper and the experiments those enzymes participated with the corresponding substrates in using chronological thinking[cite: 2, 3].
- Pass 2: Using the dossier from pass 1 Sonnet 4.6 extracts the relevant protein and activity data along with the exact verbatim evidence sentence the value was pulled from[cite: 3].
- Verification step 1: Using regex to check if the verbatim evidence sentences truly originated from the papers or were hallucinated[cite: 3].
- Verification step 2: Using simple python, the enzyme-substrate combinations from the dossier in pass 1 is compared to the extracted list in pass 2, any new proteins are flagged for review and any missing proteins are added in a notes section for LLM review[cite: 3].
- Verification step 3: A list of substrates is generated and compared against an exhaustive list of substrates to lightly flag any substrates not found in the list[cite: 3].
- Verification step 4: Sonnet 4.6 checks to see if proteins are actually tested on plastic substrates and removes any proteins that aren't with associated protein data[cite: 3].
- Rescue step 4.1: Sonnet 4.6 checks if any proteins that are tested against plastic/rubber substrates are not mentioned in the list and adds them if they are[cite: 3].
- Verification step 5: Sonnet 4.6 checks if the values are correct and originate from the evidence sentence, replacing any incorrect values with either the correct value or "Not_Found"[cite: 3].
- Rescue step 5.1: Sonnet 4.6 looks for their actual values and an accompanying evidence sentence for "Not_Found" values and verification step 4 is rerun[cite: 3].
- Verification step 6: Sonnet 4.6 ensures that each "relational pool" is correctly associated, flagging any values that aren't[cite: 3].
- Rescue step 6.1: Sonnet 4.6 takes the flagged values and associates them with the correct relational pool[cite: 3].

**Phase 2: Synthetic Control Creation and Pipeline Fidelity Validation**[cite: 3]
- Step 1: Inject synthetic protein entities, plausible activity measurements and non-degrading decoy proteins into 200 distinct academic papers[cite: 3].
- Step 2: Process the spiked corpus through pipeline iterations (v1 baseline through final v16 multi-step rescue pipeline)[cite: 3].
- Step 3: Quantify true positive capture rates, decoy false-positive acceptance rates and relational integrity linking assays to specific enzymes[cite: 3].

**Phase 3: Model Benchmarking**[cite: 4]
- Step 1: Evaluate extraction performance across Sonnet, Haiku, Opus and hybrid passes against a core truth pool (N=821 proteins)[cite: 4].
- Step 2: Compare verbatim evidence sentence overlap and semantic concordance across models[cite: 4].

**Phase 4: Production Corpus Execution**[cite: 4]
- Deploy the finalized pipeline across ~4,000 relevant papers and execute post-extraction data cleaning (collapsing duplicate entries, filtering non-numeric text and flagging low-confidence tiers)[cite: 4].

## Results & Discussion

The finalized v16 extraction architecture operates across 8 LLM steps and 3 non-LLM steps at an approximate unit cost of $0.33 per paper[cite: 4]. Across synthetic spike-in evaluations, the pipeline achieved zero decoy contamination while maintaining near-complete capture of validated entities[cite: 4]. Model benchmarking demonstrated that Sonnet provided the optimal balance of recall, precision and global metric accuracy, whereas Opus suffered from severe under-extraction (9.1% recall)[cite: 4]. Production execution across 4,049 papers yielded 51,455 high-quality measurements across diverse polymer families[cite: 4].

**Key metric**: 40,803 / 51,455 retained measurements (79%) _achieved high-confidence classification (retaining numeric value, verified unit and identified enzyme/substrate pair)_[cite: 4].

**Breakdown by category**:
**Synthetic Controls**
- 100% decoy rejection rate (no false positives)[cite: 4]
- 99.3% (152/153) Valid Spike-in Capture Rate (true positives)[cite: 4]
- 100% (152/152) Relational linkage Assays and Proteins[cite: 4]
- 98.8% (712/720) Assay Variable Extraction Rate[cite: 4]
- 98.0% (196/200) Activity Metric Extraction Rate[cite: 5]

**Cross-Model Benchmark Performance (N=821 consensus proteins)**
- Sonnet: 95.0% Precision, 87.1% Recall, 90.9% F1 Score and 97.7% Global Metric Accuracy[cite: 5]
- Haiku: 90.1% Precision, 81.5% Recall, 85.6% F1 Score and 94.4% Global Metric Accuracy[cite: 5]
- Opus: 100.0% Precision, 9.1% Recall, 16.7% F1 Score and 98.1% Global Metric Accuracy[cite: 5]
- Opus -> Sonnet Hybrid: 92.9% Precision, 80.0% Recall, 86.0% F1 Score and 98.5% Global Metric Accuracy[cite: 5]
- Opus -> Haiku Hybrid: 96.2% Precision, 76.7% Recall, 85.4% F1 Score and 95.4% Global Metric Accuracy[cite: 5]

**Production Corpus Overview (4049 papers)**
- Cleaned Entities: 27,244 experiments, 31,441 enzymes (after deduplicating 419 records) and 51,455 cleaned measurement rows (from 61,771 raw rows)[cite: 5]
- Primary Removal Drivers: 4,508 duplicate rows collapsed, 2,993 lacking measurement values, 2,354 descriptive texts, 325 missing enzymes, 111 implausible units and 25 implausible temperatures[cite: 5]
- Coverage Distribution: Median 5 experiments per paper (max 80), median 10 retained measurements per paper and median 3 enzymes per paper[cite: 5]
- Polymer Representation: PET & related polyesters (16,509 measurements; 32%), aliphatic polyesters (PCL/PLA/PHA/PBS; 15,026 measurements; 29%), polyolefins (3,429 measurements; 7%), polyurethane (2,698 measurements; 5%) and polystyrene (2,139 measurements; 4%)[cite: 5]
- Substrate Physical Form: Film (11,438), powder (3,553), solution (1,500), emulsion (963), granules (549) and nanoparticles (536)[cite: 5]

**Observations**:
- Pairwise model comparisons revealed 100% verbatim evidence sentence correlation[cite: 5].
- When different models isolated the same protein entity, their evidence spans and extracted values showed zero semantic contradiction[cite: 5].
- Strict extraction prompting prioritized precision over recall, deliberately omitting ambiguous values to prevent synthetic hallucination[cite: 5].
- Field completeness varied across experimental parameters: enzyme (100%) and substrate (99%) identification were near-universal, while assay environment parameters such as pH (40%), buffer composition (39%) and expression plasmid (8%) were less frequently reported or captured[cite: 5].
- Models rarely hallucinate past the verification steps[cite: 5].

**Follow-up questions**:
- How will ingesting unstructured supplementary tables and files impact recall rates across buffer and plasmid fields?[cite: 5]
- Will increasing input figure resolutions above 512x512 improve extraction of kinetic metrics presented strictly in graphical formats?[cite: 6]
- What downstream gains in precision can be achieved by transferring unit standardization and mathematical conversions entirely to native Python modules?[cite: 6]
- How well do the models actually compare against human data and how can they be improved?[cite: 6]
